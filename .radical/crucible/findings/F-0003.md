## Steps

1. Start the pricing service's `buildServer()` (no network needed; `app.inject`).
2. `POST /quote` with one line `{ sku: 'mug', unitPrice: 20 }`, that is, no `quantity`.
3. Control: the same line with `quantity: '0'` or `quantity: null`.

## What differs

Expected: a cart line without a valid quantity of at least one is refused with 400 and nothing is priced (rule "Quantity below one refused": "no cart is priced below zero").
Actual: the missing-quantity line answers 200 with `subtotal`, `beforeShipping` and `total` all `null` (NaN). The check `l.quantity < 1` is false for `undefined`, so the line passes and the arithmetic yields NaN. `quantity: '0'` and `quantity: null` are refused with 400 as expected, because JavaScript coerces them to 0.
Seen: 2 of 2
Where it probably comes from: services/pricing/src/quote.ts:31 (`lines.some((l) => l.quantity < 1)` accepts an absent quantity; the subtotal at :32 then becomes NaN)
A test that would catch it: services/pricing/test/quote.test.ts › quote › refuses a quantity below one. It only tests a numeric 0; it should also assert that a line with no quantity, or a non-integer or non-finite quantity, throws `BadQuantity`.
